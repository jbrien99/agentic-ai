Below is my investment decision based on the research findings available and the latest context in the provided materials.

## Chosen company: Google

### Why I chose Google
Google stands out as the strongest overall investment candidate because it combines several advantages that the other companies do not match at the same time:

1. **Scale and durability**
   - Google has massive revenue streams from search, digital advertising, cloud, and consumer products.
   - That gives it a much stronger financial base than a private AI company like Anthropic and a broader, more resilient foundation than a more narrowly AI-exposed play like Micron.

2. **Strong AI optionality**
   - Google is already a leader in frontier AI through Gemini and its broader AI research stack.
   - Unlike companies that are mainly trying to monetize AI infrastructure demand, Google can use AI across multiple large businesses: search, ads, cloud, productivity, and mobile.

3. **Cash generation and balance-sheet strength**
   - Google’s size and profitability give it flexibility to invest aggressively in AI without depending on external financing in the same way Anthropic does.
   - This lowers execution risk and makes its AI strategy more sustainable.

4. **Best risk-adjusted profile**
   - Google does face risks, especially antitrust/regulatory pressure and potential disruption to search monetization.
   - But compared with the alternatives, it offers the best mix of growth, stability, and AI exposure.

In short, Google is the best balance of long-term growth potential and lower business fragility.

---

## Companies not selected

### 1. Anthropic — Not selected
Anthropic is a compelling frontier AI company, but I did not choose it because:

- **It is highly capital intensive**
  - The research notes that it may be up to $42 billion to lease chips, which signals very heavy infrastructure dependence.
- **It depends on external compute and partners**
  - That creates meaningful operating and financing risk.
- **Private-company risk is high**
  - Even with strong model quality and enterprise interest, returns depend on monetization and continued investor support.

**Why it lost to Google:**  
Anthropic has upside, but Google offers AI exposure with much lower funding and execution risk, plus a far stronger existing business base.

---

### 2. Tencent Holdings — Not selected
Tencent is a strong company, but I did not pick it because:

- **Regulatory and geopolitical risk is significant**
  - Tencent’s exposure to China means policy and geopolitical uncertainty are persistent concerns.
- **AI investment is promising but not enough to offset risks**
  - The Oracle chip lease deal shows ambition, but it also reflects dependence on external AI infrastructure.
- **Its outlook is solid, but less clean**
  - Tencent has excellent diversification and cash generation, yet the China macro/regulatory backdrop reduces attractiveness compared with Google.

**Why it lost to Google:**  
Tencent is financially strong, but Google has a clearer global growth profile and lower regulatory/geopolitical uncertainty.

---

### 3. Oracle — Not selected
Oracle is attractive as an AI infrastructure beneficiary, but I did not choose it because:

- **Its upside is more narrow**
  - Oracle is benefiting from AI data center demand, but this is more dependent on current infrastructure spending trends.
- **It faces strong competition**
  - Hyperscalers and larger cloud rivals could pressure long-term economics.
- **The AI boom may moderate**
  - If infrastructure demand cools, Oracle’s current momentum could slow.

**Why it lost to Google:**  
Oracle has a good near-term AI infrastructure story, but Google has broader, more durable long-term upside across multiple huge businesses.

---

### 4. Micron Technology — Not selected
Micron is well positioned for AI memory demand, but I did not choose it because:

- **It is highly cyclical**
  - Memory semiconductors can produce strong upside, but they are notoriously volatile.
- **Its earnings are more sensitive to industry supply/demand swings**
  - Even with HBM and DRAM demand from AI, the business can reverse quickly if pricing weakens.
- **It is a higher-beta, less stable investment**
  - This makes it less attractive as the single best investment choice.

**Why it lost to Google:**  
Micron offers strong AI leverage, but Google provides a much better combination of upside and stability.

---

## Final decision
**Best company for investment: Google**

### One-sentence rationale
Google is the best choice because it combines dominant scale, strong cash generation, and broad AI optionality across search, cloud, and products, while carrying less execution and financing risk than the other candidates.
