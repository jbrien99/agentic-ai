# US Public EV Charging Landscape for Fleet Planning

## Public charging availability
The US now has roughly **250,000 public charging ports** across about **80,000 station locations** (AFDC / federal charging data, 2025–2026 reporting). That scale is enough to support mixed fleet use, but availability is uneven: major metro areas and interstate corridors are much better served than rural and exurban areas. For sales fleets, the practical question is less total count and more whether key routes and overnight parking locations have reliable access.

## Two major networks a fleet could rely on
**ChargePoint** is one of the largest and most established public charging networks in the US, with a very broad footprint across workplace, destination, and public Level 2 charging, plus some DC fast charging. Its strength for fleets is coverage and flexibility, especially for drivers who can top up while parked.

**Electrify America** is a major US fast-charging network focused on high-power DC charging. It is useful for sales fleets that need quick en-route charging between appointments or on long drives. Its network is smaller than ChargePoint’s overall footprint, but it is especially relevant where fast turnaround matters.

## Fleet takeaway
A sales fleet should not depend on public charging alone, but public networks are now large enough to be a meaningful fallback and trip-support option. A practical strategy is to combine depot or home charging with access to at least one broad network such as ChargePoint and one fast-charging network such as Electrify America.
